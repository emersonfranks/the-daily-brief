# Research record

Research window: 1 March through 1 September 2026 (Pacific date). Shortlist assembled from source-native arXiv records before consulting `TOPICS.md`.

## Candidate shortlist

### 1. Condensed-matter physics: a pressure pulse that leaves memory

- **Primary source:** Kristan Jensen, Alfredo Perez, and Stefan Prohazka, “Superfluid memory effect.”
- **Verified date:** 31 August 2026 (v1 submission).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31172v1
- **Finding:** Phonon scattering in a superfluid produces a far-field pressure pulse whose time integral vanishes while its first temporal moment does not. The resulting prepotential shift is permanent, and the authors estimate that the effect may be measurable in superfluid helium-4.
- **Possible pairing:** A zero-net pressure pulse in a superfluid and equal-but-separated deposits and withdrawals in a bank account. Both can finish at zero while retaining a nonzero time-weighted memory.

### 2. Theoretical ecology: resource supply selects stability or chaos

- **Primary source:** Jamila Rowland-Chandler, Akshit Goyal, and Wenying Shou, “Resource supply dynamics control stability and chaos in complex ecosystems.”
- **Verified date:** 31 August 2026 (v1 submission).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30966v1
- **Finding:** In a generalized consumer-resource model, increasingly nonreciprocal interactions drive self-renewing-resource ecosystems from stability into chaos, while externally supplied resources remain stable across a wider range and reach infeasibility without the intervening chaotic phase.
- **Possible pairing:** Microbial ecosystems and supply-chain inventories. Internal replenishment feedback can amplify disturbances, while externally metered supply can suppress the same instability.

### 3. Geophysics: earthquake slip pulses are slowly unstable

- **Primary source:** Alina Shafir, Tom Gabrieli, Yuval Tal, and Eran Bouchbinder, “Lab earthquakes confirm the theory of frictional slip pulses.”
- **Verified date:** 26 August 2026 (v1 submission).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26065v1
- **Finding:** Laboratory earthquakes reproduce a theory in which localized frictional slip pulses are categorically unstable but evolve slowly, with a measurable boundary between decaying and growing pulses.
- **Possible pairing:** Fault rupture and stop-and-go traffic. Both can carry a localized moving zone whose size and peak rate determine whether it fades or grows.

### 4. Network dynamics: a few sentinels can warn of synchrony

- **Primary source:** Yusuke Kato and Naoki Masuda, “Early warning signals for synchronization transitions from partial observations.”
- **Verified date:** 28 August 2026 (v1 submission).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28320v1
- **Finding:** In stochastic Kuramoto networks, monitoring only floor(log N) dynamically selected sentinel nodes can make two early-warning indicators approach the performance obtained by observing all N nodes.
- **Possible pairing:** Oscillator synchronization and crowd applause. A carefully chosen handful of listeners can reveal an approaching collective lock before the whole room is measured.

### 5. Astrophysical fluid dynamics: a mixing front advances by changing power laws

- **Primary source:** J. R. Fuentes, Matias Castro-Tapia, Jim Fuller, and Marcus King, “Accretion Rates and Thermohaline Convection in Polluted White Dwarfs.”
- **Verified date:** 31 August 2026 (v1 submission).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31110v1
- **Finding:** Treating thermohaline mixing as a propagating front yields mixed-layer depth and surface abundance scaling as the square root of time early on, followed by slower fractional power laws at late times; the front does not reach steady state without settling.
- **Possible pairing:** Metal mixing in a white dwarf and dye penetrating paper. Both are fronts whose apparent speed slows under diffusion-like transport, then changes regime when the environment’s stratification matters.

## Final selection and source verification

**Selected pairing:** a zero-net superfluid pressure pulse and a zero-net bank-account cash flow.

**Primary research paper:** Kristan Jensen, Alfredo Perez, and Stefan Prohazka, “Superfluid memory effect,” arXiv:2608.31172v1, submitted 31 August 2026. The arXiv abstract and submission history were opened directly and confirm the authors, date, and central result: phonon scattering produces a far-field pressure pulse with vanishing time integral, nonzero first temporal moment, and a corresponding permanent prepotential shift. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31172v1

**Independent supporting paper:** Damian H. S. Smith, Charles D. H. Williams, Adrian F. G. Wyatt, and Ruslan V. Vovk, “Energy from colliding phonon sheets in liquid 4He,” *Low Temperature Physics* 52, 407-410, published 1 April 2026. The AIP/Crossref record confirms the title, authors, date, DOI, and abstract. It reports measurements of the energy and low-energy phonon signal produced when phonon sheets collide in liquid helium-4, supporting the experimental phonon-collision substrate used by the newer theoretical proposal. https://doi.org/10.1063/10.0043138

The second source does not independently demonstrate the newly proposed memory effect; it establishes that colliding macroscopic phonon sheets and their resulting signals are experimentally measurable. The page will keep that distinction explicit.

## Provisional hypothesis

A balanced event can leave a measurable memory when its positive and negative halves occur at different times. In the proposed experiment, both the superfluid pressure trace and the account cash-flow trace end with zero net input, while their shared first temporal moment remains nonzero. The hypothesis would fail if equal-and-opposite lobes separated in time produced a first moment indistinguishable from zero, or if swapping their order did not reverse its sign.
