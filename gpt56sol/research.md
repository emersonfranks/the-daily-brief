# Research shortlist — 3 September 2026

Research window: 3 March–3 September 2026, Pacific time. This shortlist was completed before opening `TOPICS.md`. Dates and claims were checked against the linked primary records or full texts.

## Candidates

### 1. Groups that can split and merge change opinion dynamics

- **Discipline:** complex-systems physics / computational social science
- **Primary source:** Cosimo Agostinelli, Marco Mancastroppa and Alain Barrat, “Group adaptation drives opinion dynamics in higher-order networks,” published **26 May 2026**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/gcz4-wwb3) · [open paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19684)
- **Finding:** In the authors’ bounded-confidence hypergraph model, internally compatible groups average their opinions while incompatible groups split and small subgroups can merge elsewhere. Allowing group membership to adapt prevents the low-tolerance fragmentation found in a static higher-order model and restores a polarization-to-consensus transition.
- **Interactive possibility:** run pairwise, fixed-group and adaptive-group models side by side, then map group fission, coalescence and internal homogenization onto droplets.
- **Boundary:** this is modeling research, not an empirical claim that real conversations obey the model.

### 2. Hidden crack topology controls Bennu’s heat response

- **Discipline:** planetary science / geophysics
- **Primary source:** A. J. Ryan et al., “Low thermal inertia of carbonaceous asteroid Bennu driven by cracks observed in returned samples,” published **17 March 2026**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68505-1) · [open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12996602/)
- **Finding:** Measurements and tomography of returned samples show fine pores lowering local thermal inertia and tortuous cracks interrupting transport over larger scales. Those cracks explain how boulder-rich Bennu can heat and cool like a much looser surface.
- **Interactive possibility:** remove equal numbers of bonds from a heat lattice in random, straight and branching patterns, then compare transport with a damaged communication network.
- **Boundary:** a two-dimensional lattice can test a topology claim, not reproduce the paper’s three-dimensional sample measurements.

### 3. Ryugu carries all five canonical nucleobases

- **Discipline:** astrochemistry / prebiotic chemistry
- **Primary source:** Toshiki Koga, Yasuhiro Oba, Yoshinori Takano, Hiroshi Naraoka, Nanako O. Ogawa, Kazunori Sasaki, Hajime Sato, Toshihiro Yoshimura and Naohiko Ohkouchi, “A complete set of canonical nucleobases in the carbonaceous asteroid (162173) Ryugu,” published **16 March 2026**. [DOI and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-026-02791-z)
- **Finding:** The authors detected adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine and uracil. Among mineralogically similar Ryugu, Bennu and Orgueil material, purine-to-pyrimidine ratio was negatively correlated with ammonia abundance, consistent with a shared pathway whose output depends on parent-body conditions.
- **Interactive possibility:** let two product families compete for common inputs in a reduced reaction/manufacturing network.
- **Boundary:** the reported relationship is correlational; ammonia alone was not shown to determine the ratio.

### 4. Cadmium adaptation rotates a mutation spectrum

- **Discipline:** evolutionary biology / ecotoxicology
- **Primary source:** Nathan Keith et al., “Discovery and Evaluation of Cadmium-Adapted *Daphnia pulex* Genotypes in a Region of Historical Mining Reveals Adaptation Protects the Germline From Cadmium-Induced Mutations,” substantially revised **30 March 2026**, accepted 10 April and published online **27 April 2026**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/mec.70357) · [open full text](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC13113238)
- **Finding:** Cadmium affected corresponding genomic regions in adapted and nonadapted genotypes, but single-nucleotide mutation rates and some mutation classes shifted in opposite directions. Increased metallothionein copy number and expression were consistent with improved sequestration.
- **Interactive possibility:** expose unprotected and hardened systems to the same disturbance and show that hardening changes the distribution of failures, not only their count.
- **Boundary:** the result is a genotype-by-environment reversal, not proof that adaptation makes a germline mutation-proof.

### 5. Ultrafast quenching can arrest phase separation

- **Discipline:** materials science / nonequilibrium thermodynamics
- **Primary source:** Lingxiao Wang, Kai Yin, Jianqiang Xiao, Xinghao Song, Jiaqing Pei, Jun He and Ji-An Duan, “Femtosecond laser synthesis of multiscale high-entropy alloys/graphene composites for high-performance Joule heating,” published **3 March 2026**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70162-3) · [open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12957300/)
- **Finding:** A roughly 350-femtosecond pulse produced high-entropy-alloy nanoparticles on laser-induced graphene. The authors attribute uniform solid-solution formation to extremely rapid heating and quenching that fixes the mixed state before phase separation and coalescence.
- **Interactive possibility:** use one cooling schedule for a phase-separation grid and a combinatorial optimizer, revealing schedule-dependent lock-in.
- **Boundary:** the paper labels its classical heat-diffusion rates qualitative because the model omits initial electron–lattice nonequilibrium.

### 6. Density boosts improve a Ramsey-number upper bound

- **Discipline:** mathematics / combinatorics
- **Primary source:** Marcelo Campos, Simon Griffiths, Robert Morris and Julian Sahasrabudhe, “An exponential improvement for diagonal Ramsey,” issue publication **1 May 2026**. [journal page](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2026/203-3/p04) · [open manuscript](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.09521)
- **Finding:** The authors prove that some positive epsilon gives an upper bound below the classical \(4^k\)-type exponential rate for all sufficiently large \(k\). Their book algorithm tracks structured common neighborhoods and sometimes uses density-boost steps rather than locally greedy selection.
- **Interactive possibility:** compare greedy progress with deliberate investment in future viable choices on small red/blue graphs and in adaptive scheduling.
- **Boundary:** small browser graphs can explain the algorithmic intuition but cannot establish the asymptotic theorem.

### 7. Mind-wandering correlations depend on task and observation

- **Discipline:** neuroscience / cognitive science
- **Primary source:** Kara Parsons, Hannah L. Filmer, Shane E. Ehrhardt, Matilda Gordon, Reuben Rideaux, Paul E. Dux and Natasha Matthews, “Neurochemical Correlates of Mind-Wandering and Meta-Awareness,” published online **4 March 2026**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1054-25.2026) · [indexed record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/article/MED/41781321)
- **Finding:** In 46 participants measured with 7-T magnetic-resonance spectroscopy, associations varied by region, task, intentionality and whether wandering was self-caught or probe-caught. The authors’ conclusion is conditional on task demands and method of detection.
- **Interactive possibility:** run one latent process through self-alert and external-probe observation filters, like monitoring a background computer service.
- **Boundary:** the study is correlational and does not show that changing GABA or glutamate causes mind-wandering.

### 8. Large changes in both trial arms can leave a small causal contrast

- **Discipline:** clinical medicine / trial methodology
- **Primary source:** Rolf Wachter et al. and the PVI-SHAM-AF Investigators, “Catheter ablation for symptomatic atrial fibrillation (PVI-SHAM-AF): a randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled, multicentre trial,” published **30 August 2026**. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01558-8) · [trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05119231)
- **Finding:** Quality-of-life scores improved in both the ablation and sham arms, while the estimated between-group difference in change was not statistically significant for the specified six-month endpoint.
- **Interactive possibility:** pair a sham-controlled clinical trial with a product A/B test in which both groups receive a conspicuous onboarding change but only one gets the active backend feature.
- **Boundary:** the endpoint does not establish absence of rhythm-control or longer-term benefits.

## Strongest pre-ledger options

1. **Adaptive groups ↔ droplets:** the source supplies explicit update rules and a clean comparison among pairwise, static-group and adaptive-group dynamics.
2. **Bennu cracks ↔ damaged networks:** a deterministic transport model can isolate topology while holding the number of broken bonds constant.
3. **Ultrafast alloy quenching ↔ optimization lock-in:** the same schedule can drive both panels, though the reduced simulation must be labeled an analogy rather than a reproduction.

## Independent-source verification for the leading options

- **Adaptive groups:** Agostinelli, Mancastroppa and Barrat’s 26 May 2026 [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19684) defines split, merge and averaging rules and reports the restored transition. Alvarez-Rodriguez et al., “Evolutionary dynamics of higher-order interactions in social networks,” published **4 January 2021**, independently establishes that explicit group interactions can change macroscopic phase behavior, without replicating the newer split–merge result. [Independent open paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.10313)
- **Bennu cracks:** Ryan et al.’s 17 March 2026 [primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12996602/) combines returned-sample measurements, tomography and scaling. Daniel Stolte’s NASA/University of Arizona article, “Asteroid Bennu’s Rugged Surface Baffled NASA. We Finally Know Why,” also published **17 March 2026**, independently explains the pre-arrival thermal mismatch and how crack-informed scaling reconciled it. It is institutional coverage of the same study, not replication. [NASA coverage](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/osiris-rex/asteroid-bennus-rugged-surface-baffled-nasa-we-finally-know-why/)
- **Ultrafast quenching:** Wang et al.’s 3 March 2026 [primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12957300/) attributes alloy fixation to a rapid thermal cycle. Deng et al., “Phase controlled synthesis of transition metal carbide nanocrystals by ultrafast flash Joule heating,” published **11 January 2022**, independently supports the kinetic principle that rapid heating and cooling can select or preserve phases, but not the 2026 composite’s performance figures. [Independent open paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8752793/)

## Provisional hypothesis and falsifier

For the Bennu-inspired candidate: **when the number of broken connections is held constant, arranging them into a barrier should delay transport more than scattering them, in both a heat lattice and an abstract message network.** The reduced model would falsify this if its barrier pattern does not produce a longer far-edge arrival time than every tested scattered pattern over the declared deterministic seeds.

## Measurement correction

The provisional plan used half-strength arrival within 2,400 steps. It failed: both patterns remained below 0.5 during that window. The shipped question therefore uses the first step when the far edge reaches **0.2**, an observable early-arrival threshold declared consistently in the solver, interface and claims. Over seeds 3, 11, 29, 47 and 83, scattered cuts reached 0.2 in 1,242–1,272 steps; the seam reached it in 1,754 steps.
