02 / The connection

Damage is not
a quantity.
It is a topology.

A crack in a boulder and a missing route in a network obey the same reduced graph rule: transport must detour around broken connections.

The analogy here is mathematical, not empirical. Ryan and colleagues measured cracks suppressing heat transport in returned Bennu samples. This page applies a simplified diffusion equation to both rock and relays; it does not claim asteroid heat and internet traffic have been observed as one phenomenon.

What would change our mind? If a near-seam did not take longer to cross than every equal-count scattered pattern over our five declared seeds, the page’s thesis would fail.

03 / Why Bennu changed the question

101955

A boulder can behave like dust.

Before OSIRIS-REx arrived, Bennu’s rapid heating and cooling suggested a surface of small grains. The spacecraft found boulders. Returned samples supplied the missing structure: fine pores and tortuous cracks impede heat flow while the rock remains intact.

04 / Open the mechanismHow the reduced model works +
01

One graph

A 34 × 20 rectangular graph holds a scalar from zero to one. The left edge is maintained at one. Every step exchanges a fraction of the difference between connected neighbors.

02

Equal damage

Every pattern removes exactly 16 graph edges. The slider gradually organizes those cuts along the center line, leaving detours around the seam’s ends.

03

Two readings

The rock renderer calls the scalar temperature and the missing edges cracks. The network renderer calls the same scalar message concentration and the same edges failed routes.

04

Deliberate limit

This is two-dimensional isotropic diffusion, not Bennu mineral physics or packet routing. Our planned half-strength measure exceeded the interactive window, so the shipped, declared question is early arrival at 0.2.
