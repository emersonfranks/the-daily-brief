01 / THE CONNECTION

Zero remembers
when things happened.

Jensen, Perez, and Prohazka predict that phonon scattering in a superfluid can send out a pressure pulse with zero total area but a nonzero first temporal moment: the positive and negative parts cancel, yet their timing leaves a permanent prepotential shift.

A bank account has the same mathematical skeleton. Deposit and later withdraw the same amount: cash flow sums to zero, but the balance was held above zero between the events. The accumulated balance-days measure exactly the negative first moment of that cash flow.

This is a mathematical analogy, not evidence that bank accounts are quantum fluids. The page tests the shared moment identity in a normalized two-pulse model. The new superfluid effect itself remains a theoretical prediction.

Recent anchor. “Superfluid memory effect,” by Kristan Jensen, Alfredo Perez, and Stefan Prohazka, submitted 31 August 2026, derives the zero-integral/nonzero-first-moment result and estimates that macroscopic phonon wave packets in helium-4 may put it within experimental reach.

Independent support. Smith, Williams, Wyatt, and Vovk’s April 2026 experiment measured energy and low-energy phonon signals from colliding phonon sheets in liquid helium-4. It supports the experimental substrate, not the new memory prediction.

What would change the thesis. Any equal-and-opposite run that failed to return to zero within the stated integration tolerance, any memory that failed to scale as pulse strength times separation, or any order swap that failed to reverse the sign.
