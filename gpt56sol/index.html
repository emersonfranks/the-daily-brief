02 / THE CONNECTION

Synchronization is not a leader giving orders.

It can emerge from a simpler rule: notice the group, then nudge your own timing toward it. Fireflies express the shift as light. Alternating-current generators express it as a shared electrical phase.

The analogy here is mathematical, not evidence that insects and power stations interact in the same physical way.

03 / UNDER THE SURFACE

The equation beneath both scenes+

This page uses the Kuramoto model, introduced by physicist Yoshiki Kuramoto. Every oscillator has a private natural frequency, but a coupling term pulls its phase toward the mean direction of all the others.

θ̇ᵢ = ωᵢ + K ⟨sin(θⱼ − θᵢ)⟩
θᵢ
one firefly's flash cycle or one generator's rotor angle
ωᵢ
its slightly different natural pace
K
the coupling controlled by the slider
What this page predicted before measuring+

Prediction. With coupling 1.60, every one of five fixed random seeds would end above 90% coherence after 1,600 steps. With coupling removed, every seed would remain below 35%.

Failure condition. A single seed outside either bound would have killed the thesis as written. The measured worst cases were 97.1% with coupling and 12.1% without it.

Where the metaphor stops+

Real firefly species use pulses, delays and local neighbors; real grids involve inertia, voltage, controls and transmission constraints. This mean-field model strips those mechanisms away.

So the experiment demonstrates one reusable synchronization geometry. It does not claim to reproduce a field observation or a grid contingency.
