02 / THE CONNECTION

Synchrony is not a conductor. It is a negotiation.

In this stripped-down model, no oscillator knows the correct time. Each advances at its own natural rate and makes one local correction toward the population’s average phase.

That feedback is enough. Above the tested coupling, difference becomes correction faster than individual timing can recreate difference. The field flashes together; the grid’s rotors turn together. These are two interpretations of the same Kuramoto phase equation, not evidence that real fireflies and power grids are empirically interchangeable.

dθᵢ / dt = ωᵢ + K · sin(Ψ − θᵢ)
θᵢ one oscillator’s phase ωᵢ its natural timing K how strongly it listens Ψ the group’s mean phase

03 / UNDER THE SURFACE

Why the pictures are identical+

Both canvases receive the same array of 36 phases. The left translates phase into a pulse of light; the right translates it into a rotor angle. Their coherence readouts must match because the difference is visual vocabulary, not mathematics.

What the experiment actually tests+

Five fixed populations each contain natural frequencies drawn from 0.72 to 1.28 model units. The suite compares weak coupling at K = 0.08 with strong coupling at K = 1.35 after 18 simulated seconds, then randomizes 40% of phases and measures ten seconds of recovery.

The prediction would fail if any shipped seed gained no more than 0.55 coherence, or if any disturbed population recovered to no more than 0.90 coherence. Those thresholds were fixed against the five-seed measurement, not a single preferred run.

Where the analogy stops+

Yoshiki Kuramoto’s phase model is the named mathematical foundation. It is widely used to study synchronization, while Steven Strogatz and collaborators connected coupled-oscillator theory to collective biological timing. Real fireflies use sensory and species-specific response rules. Real grids use richer swing equations, controls, transmission constraints, and protection systems. This page tests a shared reduced model only.
