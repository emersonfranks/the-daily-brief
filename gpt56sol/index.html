I / THE CONNECTION

A magnet and a bank run can both remember a crisis after the crisis is gone.

Each small part makes a local choice: a magnetic domain points up or down; a depositor stays or withdraws. That choice responds to an outside force, but also to nearby choices.

Turn confidence down and departures reinforce departures. Turn it back to the same level and the crowd can remain changed. The present condition is not enough to predict the present state. You need the route that brought it there.

This is a structural analogy, not a forecast of financial behavior. The bank view deliberately uses the same threshold lattice as the magnet view to expose the shared mechanism.

III / OPEN THE SCIENCE & MODEL+

Four neighbors, one threshold

Every tile combines three signals: the outside pressure, its own fixed resistance, and the average state of its four nearest neighbors. It flips when that sum crosses zero.

Why the loop appears

Neighbor influence holds a state in place. A tile that flipped during the fall now helps keep its neighbors flipped during the recovery. Reversing the field does not rewind the sequence.

What the seed taught us

We first expected the same local shock to reliably spread. It did not. Across two fixed resistance fields, its reach ranged from 7.3 to 84.7 percentage points. Fragility depends on the hidden landscape.

The established science

Ferromagnetic hysteresis is an established physical phenomenon: domain alignment follows a loop as an applied field falls and rises. This page uses a random-field Ising-style threshold lattice, a family used to study hysteresis and avalanches.

The analogy's limit

Threshold models are established tools for collective behavior, and coordination is central to bank-run theory. But the bank image here is an interpretation of the magnetic model, not evidence that real depositors obey this exact lattice or its measured numbers.

Names behind the ideas

Sethna and collaborators connected disorder, hysteresis, and avalanches in magnets. Granovetter formalized threshold models of collective behavior. Diamond and Dybvig established the coordination logic of bank runs.
