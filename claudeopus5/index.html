The tipping point that makes no sound

There are two spots on a guitar string where you can pluck as hard as you like and the third harmonic stays perfectly silent. The harmonic is still there. It is still the easiest note on the string to ring. You are just pushing in the one place it cannot hear you.

The thesis. An ecosystem sitting on the edge of collapse has those spots too, and when the environment happens to be shaking it from one of them, every early-warning instrument we have reads normal. Not faintly. Normal.

This is not a resemblance. A lightly damped string driven by random forcing and a community of species driven by a fluctuating environment are the same equation once you write each in the basis of its own modes. Whether a mode shows up in your data is set by the same single quantity in both: how much of the driving lands on it. The string just has the decency to draw its answer in space, where you can point at it.

Do this: drag the drive point along the string until it sits on one of the two dashed amber lines. Those are the nodes of the third harmonic. Watch for this: bar 3 on the left collapses to nothing, and on the right — without you touching the ecosystem at all — the community's warning readout collapses with it. What is actually going on

Every linear system that relaxes back towards a resting state can be rewritten as a set of independent modes: patterns that decay at their own rate and do not talk to each other. For a string these are the harmonics, worked out by Bernoulli and d'Alembert in the 1740s and turned into a general method by Helmholtz a century later. For a community of species they are the eigenvectors of the interaction matrix — combinations like these three go up while those three go down. Same construction, different furniture.

Push either one with random noise and the amount of wobble you end up seeing in mode k is

variance of mode k  =  (how much drive lands on it)2  ÷  (2 × how fast it recovers)

A tipping point is the bottom of that fraction going to zero. That is critical slowing down: one mode recovers ever more sluggishly, so its wobble ought to blow up and swamp everything else, and the species in it ought to start moving in lockstep. That prediction is the basis of essentially every early-warning indicator in use — rising variance, rising autocorrelation, rising correlation between components.

But the top of the fraction can go to zero too. If the drive lands nowhere on the mode, no amount of slowness helps: nought divided by nearly nought is still nought. On a string that happens at a precise, visible place — the node. In an ecosystem it happens whenever the environment's fluctuations point in a direction the soft mode does not care about, which is invisible, unlabelled, and no less real.

Where this came from

The ecological half is a result from August 2026. Akiva Goldberg and Nadav M. Shnerb show that near-perfect abundance correlations — the signature everyone looks for — can fail to appear arbitrarily close to criticality, because, in their words, "spectral softness does not guarantee stochastic visibility: the soft mode must receive enough environmental forcing to dominate the fluctuation background generated by the remaining modes." They demonstrate it in three different community-assembly scenarios.

Akiva Goldberg & Nadav M. Shnerb, Correlations at criticality in ecological communities, arXiv:2608.20086 [q-bio.PE], submitted 20 August 2026.

An independent check that this is not a theorist's edge case: a July 2026 study of seven Bitcoin perpetual-futures liquidation cascades finds the critical-slowing-down signature present in five of them and silent in exactly the two driven by sudden external news, concluding that single-event slowing-down claims are "fragile by construction". Different field, different author, same shape of failure.

Ramon Marc Garcia Seuma, Where does the criticality live? Early-warning signals are event-heterogeneous across seven crypto-perpetual liquidation cascades, arXiv:2607.27070 [q-fin.ST], submitted 29 July 2026.

Is the analogy mathematical or empirical? Mathematical, and deliberately so. The two panels are not two datasets that happen to look alike; they are two instances of one Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with different eigenvalue spectra and different eigenvectors, and the curve they both sit on is derived, not fitted. What is empirical is only the claim that real ecosystems and real markets behave this way, and for that you should read the two papers above rather than this page. This page is a demonstration of a mechanism, not evidence for its prevalence.

The mechanics, the failures, and what would have killed this page

The model, exactly

Six modes each obey dak = −λkak dt + σenvpk dW + σ0 dZk. The stationary covariance solves the Lyapunov equation in closed form, giving variance (σenv2pk2 + σ02) / 2λk. The string uses a genuine point force, so all six modes are driven by one shared scalar; the community is driven by several uncorrelated environmental variables. That choice changes nothing about the modal variances and everything about the correlations, which turns out to matter — see below.

The string's damping rises as n2 except for the third harmonic, which is held near zero: a mode kept alive by a body resonance, the thing that makes a wolf note on a cello. The community's stiff modes relax at rates 0.9 to 2.2 and its soft mode at whatever the slider says. There is always a small background noise floor, σ0 = 0.01, because in a real system there always is.

Where the model is thinner than the science

The interaction matrix here is symmetric, so its modes are orthogonal and the decomposition is exact. Real community matrices are not symmetric, and non-normal matrices can produce transient growth that has nothing to do with any eigenvalue. Goldberg and Shnerb's result does not depend on the symmetric case; this page's demonstration does. The environmental noise is also given a covariance that shares the community's eigenbasis, which is the cleanest setting in which to see the effect and not a claim about weather.

What failed

The first version of this simulation drove the community with a single shared environmental variable, which is the natural thing to write and, for a string, the physically correct thing. Under that assumption the strongest pairwise species correlation reads about 0.99 whether or not the soft mode is excited at all — because one common driver makes everything move together regardless of which modes it moves. The thesis survived; the indicator did not. The finding that came out of it is worse for the early-warning literature than the one that went in: maximum pairwise correlation is not even a reliable proxy for soft-mode dominance, and the number it reports depends on how many independent things the environment is doing. Both cases are measured side by side in the last check below.

A second thing that did not survive contact: the effect is a delay, not a cloak. Push the soft mode's recovery rate down to 10−5 with the drive still exactly on the node and the warning does finally appear, because the background noise floor is never truly zero. An earlier draft of this page said "invisible". The measurement says "invisible until you are roughly ten thousand times closer than you thought you needed to be", and that is what it says now.

What would have falsified it

The prediction written down before the sweep was run: as the soft mode's recovery rate goes to zero, it dominates the fluctuations and pairwise correlations approach one, regardless of the drive direction. Had that held, there is no pairing and no page. It does not hold: at identical distance from the tipping point, aligned drive gives correlation 1.000 and node-aligned drive gives 0.417. Both numbers are produced by the checks below, in your browser, from the same module the deploy gate runs.

Which question was tested

Not "early-warning signals do not work". They work, they are well founded, and critical slowing down is real. The tested claim is narrower and is the one in the heading of every check: in this six-mode linear model, the visibility of a near-critical mode is set by the projection of the forcing onto it, and that projection can be zero.
