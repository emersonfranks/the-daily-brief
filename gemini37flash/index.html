The Core Thesis

When a flexible filament is assembled from bistable units that each prefer to twist left or right, a microscopic chiral nudge does not act in isolation—it triggers a 1D cooperative chain reaction. By coupling adjacent units through steric hindrance or elastic stiffness, domain walls (topological perversion nodes) are swept to the boundaries and annihilated, amplifying a minute chemical solvent bias into a single macroscopic spiral.

System A: Chiral Graphene Nanoribbon

Unit: A fused [4]helicene aromatic monomer with bistable left ($M$) and right ($P$) spiral conformations.

Nudge: Chiral solvent molecule (β-pinene from citrus peel/pine trees) exerting a weak differential solvation free energy ($h$).

Cooperative Action: Steric hindrance between adjacent fused benzene rings forces neighbors to share handedness ($J$). At low temperature, the entire polymer chain acts as a single macroscopic optical switch emitting circularly polarized luminescence (CPL).

System B: Climbing Plant Tendril

Unit: An elastic botanical ribbon segment with cellular differential turgor pressure and internal pre-strain.

Nudge: Unilateral sunlight, touch sensation, or mechanical twisting torque ($h$).

Cooperative Action: Continuous elastic torsional stiffness minimizes curvature energy. If the tendril is anchored at both ends, topological conservation prevents total unwinding, trapping stable perversion nodes (reversals of coiling direction discovered by Darwin).

Deep Structural Mechanics

1. The 1D Cooperative Ising Hamiltonian & Sergeants-and-Soldiers Effect

The conformational state of a polymer ribbon with $N$ monomer units is described by a 1D Hamiltonian:

H(s) = -J ∑ s_i s_{i+1} - h ∑ s_i

where $s_i = \pm 1$ represents the local enantiomeric handedness ($M$ vs $P$), $J$ is the steric neighbor coupling energy, and $h$ is the chiral chemical potential difference imparted by the chiral solvent (β-pinene).

Without coupling ($J = 0$), the thermal expectation of a single unit is $\langle s \rangle = \tanh(h / k_B T)$. For weak solvent biases ($h = 0.08 J$), a solitary monomer has almost no handedness preference ($\approx 8\%$). But when $J > k_B T$, the correlation length $\xi(T) = -1 / \ln(\tanh(J/k_B T))$ grows exponentially. When $\xi(T) \ge N$, the entire ribbon undergoes a cooperative transition into a single domain, amplifying the enantiomeric excess to $> 99\%$. This supramolecular phenomenon is famously known as the Sergeants-and-Soldiers effect and Majority Rule.

2. Topological Perversion Kinks & Soliton Dynamics in Tendrils

In botanical climbing plants (such as cucumber and passionflower tendrils, studied by Charles Darwin in 1865 and formalized by Goriely & Tabor in 1998), a filament that attaches at both ends cannot add net topological turns without boundary slip.

As internal differential contraction shrinks the strand, it relieves strain by forming left-handed and right-handed helical coils separated by a perversion node—an inflection point where curvature passes through zero and chirality reverses. Mathematically, these nodes behave as topological solitons (domain walls). When a chiral torque is applied to an unpinned strand, the domain wall experiences an effective drift velocity $v \propto h$, sweeping out of the open end and leaving a pure uniform helix.

3. Temperature Breakdown & Thermal Racemization

In the September 2026 experiment by Ikai et al., the graphene nanoribbon displayed near-perfect chiral switching at cryogenic temperatures (-90°C), but the switching efficiency weakened as the system approached room temperature.

Our simulation confirms this exact thermal breakdown: as thermal energy $k_B T$ approaches and exceeds the domain wall formation energy $2J$, entropy favors the spontaneous nucleation of wandering perversion kinks. The correlation length $\xi$ shrinks below the ribbon length $N$, partitioning the molecule into a chaotic mixture of microscopic left and right domains, reducing the net optical polarization to zero.
