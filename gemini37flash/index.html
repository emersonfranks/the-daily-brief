Core Universal Thesis
Optimal transport does not require a central architect. When conduit conductance expands in response to hydrodynamic flux and atrophies under stagnation, decentralized networks autonomously discover geodesic shortest paths, balance congestion, and self-heal around severed arteries. Both the amoeba's protoplasmic cytoplasm and the Internet's dynamic routing fabric are manifestations of this exact same conductance relaxation principle.

📐 The Mathematical Mechanics: Kirchhoff-Poiseuille Relaxation ▼

The mathematical foundation connecting biological transport and electrical/data networks is the Tero-Kobayashi-Nakagaki model (2007). Consider a network of nodes $V$ connected by tubular edges $E$. Each edge $(i,j)$ has length $L_{ij}$ and conductivity $D_{ij}$.

1. Poiseuille Flow & Kirchhoff's Current Law

Assuming laminar viscous flow, the flux $Q_{ij}$ through conduit $(i,j)$ is proportional to the pressure gradient $\Delta p_{ij} = p_i - p_j$:

Q_{ij} = \frac{D_{ij}}{L_{ij}} (p_i - p_j)

At every intermediate node $i$, fluid is neither created nor destroyed. The sum of all incoming and outgoing fluxes equals the externally injected current $I_i$ ($+I_0$ at sources, $-I_0$ at sinks, $0$ at junctions):

\sum_{j \in N(i)} \frac{D_{ij}}{L_{ij}} (p_i - p_j) = I_i \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{K} \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{I}

where $\mathbf{K}$ is the symmetric, positive semi-definite graph Laplacian conductance matrix.

2. Flux-Driven Conductance Evolution

Tubes adapt over time according to biological shear stress feedback. The diameter grows with the absolute flux carried, balanced by natural metabolic decay rate $r$:

\frac{d D_{ij}}{dt} = |Q_{ij}|^\gamma - r D_{ij}

Here, $\gamma$ is the non-linear feedback exponent. This non-linear ordinary differential equation governs the global topological phase transition of the network.

🔀 The Bifurcation Theorem: Why γ Governs Resilience vs Efficiency ▼

The exponent $\gamma$ controls the fundamental economic trade-off in network architecture:

  • Sublinear Feedback ($\gamma < 1$): Marginal reinforcement diminishes as flow grows. The network maintains multiple redundant parallel pathways, guaranteeing high fault tolerance against unexpected line cuts (crucial for resilient Internet topologies).
  • Linear Feedback ($\gamma = 1$): Exponential divergence between asymmetric loops. Proven by Bonifaci et al. (2012) to compute the geodesic shortest path in polynomial time.
  • Superlinear Feedback ($\gamma > 1$): Winner-take-all dynamics. Alternative loops are rapidly starved to minimum floor conductivity, collapsing the graph into a minimal spanning or Steiner tree with zero loop redundancy (optimizing infrastructure construction cost).

⚠️ Numerical Limits & Discrepancy Analysis ▼

In continuous mathematics, dormant veins can decay to zero conductivity ($D \to 0$). However, if $D = 0$ everywhere except the active path, the graph Laplacian becomes permanently disconnected, preventing the system from ever exploring alternative paths when an artery is severed.

To mirror real biological tissue (where basal plasmodial sheets maintain a microscopic dormant vascular bed), our implementation enforces a physical floor $D_{\text{min}} = 10^{-3}$. This small residual conductance allows pressure waves to propagate through dormant conduits upon severance, providing the backpressure trigger that drives autonomous self-healing.
