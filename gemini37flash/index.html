The Core Thesis

In classical equilibrium thermodynamics, when components of a binary mixture attract one another, the system inevitably undergoes unconstrained phase separation (coarsening): microscopic domains merge into macroscopic droplets over time (Lifshitz-Slyozov / Ostwald ripening), minimizing free energy until the entire mixture collapses into a single static condensate or segregated bulk phase.

However, when microscopic interactions violate Newton’s third law of action and reaction ($F_{AB} \neq -F_{BA}$)—as occurs when particle A is hydrodynamically attracted to B while B is repelled by A—the system can no longer be described by a conservative potential or free energy functional.

Instead, broken action-reaction symmetry triggers a non-Hermitian dynamical phase transition. The unequal pairwise forces exert continuous internal torque and self-propulsion along the separation vector, accelerating multi-particle clusters into traveling pursuit states. This persistent internal shear dynamically arrests coarsening: traveling clusters collide, fragment, and reorganize at a finite, non-equilibrium length scale, permanently preventing static thermodynamic collapse.

Exactly the same mathematical bifurcation governs spatial ecology: when predators climb prey density gradients ($+\chi_{pred} \nabla N$) while prey evade predator gradients ($-\chi_{prey} \nabla P$), the nonreciprocal spatial cross-taxis halts localized over-consumption and monolithic extinction, sustaining perpetual rotating pursuit spirals and stable non-equilibrium biodiversity.

Under the Hood: Mathematics & Mechanics

1. Breaking Newton's Third Law & Nonreciprocal Langevin Dynamics

Consider two colloidal particles $i \in \text{Species A}$ and $j \in \text{Species B}$ separated by distance vector $\mathbf{r}_{ij} = \mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i$. In an overdamped viscous solvent with drag coefficient $\gamma$:

\gamma \frac{d\mathbf{x}_i}{dt} = \mathbf{F}_{AB}(\mathbf{r}_{ij}) + \boldsymbol{\xi}_i(t) = +f_A(r) \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij} + \boldsymbol{\xi}_i(t)
\gamma \frac{d\mathbf{x}_j}{dt} = \mathbf{F}_{BA}(\mathbf{r}_{ji}) + \boldsymbol{\xi}_j(t) = +f_B(r) \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij} + \boldsymbol{\xi}_j(t)

When $f_A \neq -f_B$, the net internal force on the two-body center of mass is non-zero:

\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{F}_{AB} + \mathbf{F}_{BA} = (f_A + f_B) \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij} \neq \mathbf{0} \implies \mathbf{v}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{f_A + f_B}{2\gamma} \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ij}

This autonomous pair propulsion violates the classical action-reaction principle without requiring external energy gradients or chemical self-electrophoresis fuels, deriving persistent motility entirely from asymmetric inter-particle wake couplings.

2. Non-Hermitian Cahn-Hilliard & Cross-Taxis PDEs

At the continuum level, the coupled density fields $\phi_A(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and $\phi_B(\mathbf{x}, t)$ follow nonreciprocal Cahn-Hilliard conservation laws:

\partial_t \phi_A = \nabla \cdot \left[ M_A \nabla \left( \frac{\delta \mathcal{F}}{\delta \phi_A} + \alpha_{AB} \phi_B \right) \right]
\partial_t \phi_B = \nabla \cdot \left[ M_B \nabla \left( \frac{\delta \mathcal{F}}{\delta \phi_B} - \alpha_{BA} \phi_A \right) \right]

When the cross-coupling matrix is asymmetric ($\alpha_{AB} \neq \alpha_{BA}$), the linearized dynamical matrix $\mathcal{M}(k)$ becomes non-Hermitian. Above a critical nonreciprocity threshold $\Delta_c$, the eigenvalues $\omega(k)$ pass through an exceptional point and acquire non-zero imaginary parts ($\text{Im}(\omega) \neq 0$). This converts the stationary spinodal growth mode into propagating traveling waves with dispersion velocity $c \propto \sqrt{|\alpha_{AB}\alpha_{BA}|}$, dynamically halting Ostwald ripening.
