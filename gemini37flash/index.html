The Underlying Mathematics: 2D Site Percolation

First formulated mathematically by Broadbent and Hammersley in 1957 to model fluid flow through porous gas masks, percolation theory provides the foundation for statistical geometric phase transitions.

P∞(p) ∼ (p - pc)β   for p > pc,   where β = 5/36 ≈ 0.1389 (in 2D)

In a 2D square lattice with nearest-neighbor coupling, the critical site occupation threshold is pc = 0.592746.... Below this point, all clusters are exponentially bounded in size with mean radius ξ(p) ∼ |p - pc|-ν (ν = 4/3). At exactly p = pc, cluster sizes follow a scale-free power law P(s) ∼ s-τ with Fisher exponent τ = 187/91 ≈ 2.055.

Domain Mapping: What One Unit Represents

In Wildfire & Epidemic Modeling: Each occupied cell represents a mature, combustible tree (or susceptible host). Adjacent occupied cells transmit heat/infection with probability 1.0. A single tree removed acts as a firebreak or immune barrier. Below 59% density, large wildfires are geometrically impossible regardless of ignition intensity.

In Conductor-Dielectric Composites: Each occupied cell represents a metallic grain with unit conductance G0 = 1.0 S, surrounded by insulating polymer matrix. Finite-Size Effects & Measured Simulation Artifacts

In an infinite thermodynamic lattice (L &to; ∞), the transition from non-percolating to percolating is a true non-analytic singularity: Π(p) is 0 for p < pc and 1 for p > pc.

On our finite simulation grid (L = 40), boundary effects smooth the step into an S-shaped sigmoid. At p = 0.593 on a 40×40 grid, random sample variance produces spanning clusters in approximately 40%–80% of seeds. As you increase the lattice dimension to L = 60 or L = 100, the transition width Δp scales as L-1/ν = L-3/4, narrowing monotonically toward the theoretical singularity proven by Stanislav Smirnov (Fields Medal 2001) for conformal invariance in percolation.
