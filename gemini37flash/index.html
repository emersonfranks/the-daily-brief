Core Level: The Shared Governing Pattern

In conventional active matter (Motility-Induced Phase Separation or MIPS), particles slow down when crowded, acting like a traffic jam that grows denser and denser. But in Density-Enhanced Motility (DEM)—the inverted quorum-sensing rule recently formulated by Azizi (2026)—particles do the exact opposite: they remain motionless when solitary, and ignite into violent ballistic dispersal only when surrounded by a critical density of peers ρ ≥ ρc.

This inverted threshold triggers an identical mathematical feedback loop to feedback-regulated star formation in interstellar giant molecular clouds (McKee & Ostriker 1977, Hopkins et al. 2014). In astrophysics, cold gas collapses gravitationally until the core crosses the Jeans threshold for starburst ignition. Massive OB stars ignite, launching stellar winds and supernova blastwaves that blow the gas cloud out into a hollowed superbubble cavity—cutting off the fuel supply and quenching further star formation.

Physical Stage System A: Microbial Quorum Swarm System B: Interstellar Gas Cloud
1. Subcritical Infall Chemotactic / passive accretion: cells gather slowly into dense colony nodes (ρ < ρc). Gravitational accretion: cold interstellar gas falls inward under self-gravity (ρ < ρSF).
2. Threshold Ignition Autoinducer concentration crosses quorum threshold ρc, switching on hyper-motile flagellar propulsion. Gas density crosses star-formation threshold ρSF, triggering massive OB star formation.
3. Explosive Feedback Active cells sprint radially outward down the local density gradient (∇ρ), blasting the colony apart. Supernovae & radiation pressure unleash supersonic blastwaves, sweeping gas into an expanding shock shell.
4. Self-Starvation Cavitation The explosion hollows out the core; local density drops below ρc, freezing propulsion back to passive tumble. The core is blown clear into a low-density cavity; star formation halts due to lack of gas fuel.
5. Secondary Accretion Ring Frozen cells accumulate at the shock rim, trapping incoming migrants until central accretion restarts the cycle. Swept-up supershell cools and collapses at the perimeter, triggering secondary triggered star formation.

Deep Level: Mathematical Physics & Phase Space Mechanics

Continuum Hydrodynamics of Density-Enhanced Motility

The continuum evolution of the local particle density field ρ(&mathbf;r}, t) in both systems is governed by a non-linear advection-diffusion conservation equation with threshold-activated kinetic flux:

∂ρ/∂t = D ∇²ρ + ∇ · [γ (&mathbf;r} - &mathbf;r}_{c}) ρ] - ∇ · [ρ \mathbf{v}_{blast}(\rho)]

where the blast velocity &mathbf{v}_{blast}(\rho) exhibits a discontinuous Heaviside or sigmoidal step response:

\mathbf{v}_{blast}(\rho) = -v_{0} \frac{\nabla \rho}{|\nabla \rho|} \Theta(\rho - \rho_c)

When ρ < ρc, the system acts as a standard dissipative sink with inward drift γ, condensing into a localized peak. However, as soon as ρ ≥ ρc, the divergence term ∇ · (ρ \mathbf{v}_{blast}) explodes to positive values, driving local ∂ρ/∂t << 0. This creates a non-equilibrium limit cycle in (ρcore, ⟨v⟩) phase space rather than a stationary steady state.

Astrophysical Superbubble Analogy: The McKee-Ostriker Model

In interstellar astrophysics, the Sedov-Taylor blastwave and Weaver et al. (1977) wind-blown bubble equations dictate that an energy injection rate É into an ambient medium of density ρ0 produces a cavitation radius scaling as:

R(t) ≈ (É / ρ0)1/5 t3/5

Because the star-formation rate scales non-linearly with density via the Kennicutt-Schmidt law ΣSFR ∝ Σgas1.4, high-density cores experience catastrophic overpressure that blows away 90% of the cold gas before it can collapse into stars. This self-limiting feedback explains why galaxies convert gas into stars at an efficiency of only ~1-2% per freefall time.
