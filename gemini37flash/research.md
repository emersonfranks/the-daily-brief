### Final Selection: Candidate 2
- **Discovery Anchor:** Tomoyuki Ikai et al., "Switchable helical graphene nanoribbons controlled by chiral solvents", *Nature Communications* (September 2026). DOI: [10.1038/s41467-026-76724-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76724-9). Supported by Phys.org (September 3, 2026, [https://phys.org/news/2026-09-switchable-graphene-nanoribbon-demand.html](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-switchable-graphene-nanoribbon-demand.html)).
- **Paired System:** Biomechanical climbing plant tendrils and elastic ribbons (Goriely & Tabor, 1998; Gerbode et al., *Science* 2012; Darwin, *The Movements and Habits of Climbing Plants*, 1865).
- **System A:** A synthetic helical graphene nanoribbon switching between left-handed ($M$) and right-handed ($P$) twists when exposed to a chiral solvent nudge ($\beta$-pinene).
- **System B:** A climbing plant tendril (or twisted elastic filament) resolving mechanical torque by forming left- and right-handed helical coils separated by topological perversion kinks.
- **Universal Pattern:** 1D Cooperative Bistable Chirality and Topological Domain Wall (Perversion Kink) Annihilation. In both systems, individual units are bistable with strong neighbor coupling ($J$). A weak chiral bias ($h$) propagates through cooperative alignment, pushing domain walls (perversion kinks) to the boundaries to lock the entire filament into a macroscopic single-handed spiral, while thermal fluctuations ($k_B T$) or end-pinning constraints create stable multi-domain helical textures.
